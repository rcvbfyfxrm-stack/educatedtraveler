What you take back to your kitchen.

Three weeks at Hiroko’s bench, every class day. You leave with a sushi service you can put on your own menu.

  • The service

    An omakase you can run.

    The prep, the order, the timing. You watch Hiroko serve one, then you serve two rounds yourself.

  • The rice

    Rice you can repeat.

    Cooked and seasoned until it is steady. It is most of what you taste, so it comes back every day.

  • The fish

    Whole fish, broken down by you.

    Horse mackerel, flounder, salmon, yellowtail. Ikejime on a sea bream at Toyosu, and a bream aged four days tasted against a fresh one.

  • The kitchen you work in

    Techniques that come home with you.

    Your own knives sharpened. The tamago made on the pan you use at work. Kelp and vinegar cures you can prepare ahead.

Every day, as Hiroko set it
Week one

1 – 5 March

MON 1

Rice, and the first cut of tuna

Japanese rice, cooked and seasoned with sushi vinegar. The rice is most of what you taste, so it comes first and comes back on day two. Slicing a saku of tuna, and a tuna zuke bowl for lunch. Nikiri soy and ponzu. Knife check, and sharpening: she insists on it.

TUE 2

The yanagiba, the first nigiri

Rice again, until it is steady. The yanagiba: slicing fish for sushi. The basic steps of nigiri. Nori, cucumber cut for hosomaki, and the first hosomaki.

WED 3

A whole fish, and dashi

Horse mackerel in three pieces, sanmai-oroshi, and the skin off with the knife. Nigiri, sashimi and a roll from it. Basic dashi, and miso soup with tofu and wakame. Hosomaki practice. Gari, the pickled ginger, made from scratch.

THU 4

Shrimp, and the tamago

Three kinds of shrimp, prepared. Nigiri from them, and miso soup from their heads. Then the egg omelette, made by every chef on the non-stick pan you will use at work, and nigiri from it.

FRI 5

Salmon

A salmon fillet prepared and cut for sushi: nigiri, hosomaki, the inside-out roll. A softshell crab roll. Salmon nanban-zuke, and salmon marinated in saikyo miso.

Week two

8 – 13 March

MON 8

Yellowtail, and flounder

A yellowtail fillet cut for sushi, and aburi, seared with the torch. Last Friday’s nanban-zuke and saikyo-zuke, tasted. Buri daikon, yellowtail simmered with radish. Flounder, scaled with the knife, suki-biki.

TUE 9

Flounder, kombu-jime, the cut vegetables

Filleting the flounder. Nigiri from it, and kombu-jime, the fish cured between sheets of kelp. Usuzukuri, the thin-sliced sashimi plate. Momiji-oroshi, and cut vegetables for the plate. Mackerel, prepared. Yesterday’s buri daikon, tasted.

Evening · the whole bluefin tuna night, with sake being arranged

WED 10

Vinegar, tuna, conger eel

Gizzard shad and mackerel, cured in vinegar. A tuna plate: nigiri, zuke nigiri, a mid-thick roll, sashimi, a hand roll. Conger eel, shown by Hiroko. A whole salmon, from the fish to the piece.

THU 11

Octopus, squid, tofu

Simmered octopus. Nigiri and sashimi of gizzard shad, and bo-zushi, the mackerel pressed into a bar. Squid or cuttlefish. Tofu made from scratch. A sashimi plate of three.

Evening · sake night in Hiroko’s classroom, with the whole class idea

FRI 12

The first small service

The prep for a bar: yellowtail, red shrimp, boiled shrimp, horse mackerel. Scallop. Then a small counter practice, the chefs serving nigiri to each other, okonomi style, one piece as it is asked for. Tempura.

SAT 13

Toyosu fish market agreed

Early morning at the market. Hiroko demonstrates ikejime, then each chef who wants to practises it on a sea bream. Breakfast at the market. Sea bream aged four days, tasted against fresh.

Week three

15 – 19 March

MON 15

The bream from the market

Saturday’s sea bream from Toyosu: yushimo, hot water on the skin, for sashimi and sushi. Ushio-jiru, the clear soup from its bones. The fillings for futomaki and inari: dried shiitake, kanpyo, the inari pockets, tamago.

TUE 16

Shellfish, futomaki, a whole yellowtail

Ark shell, geoduck, surf clam. Futomaki, rolled from yesterday’s fillings. A whole yellowtail, from the fish to the piece.

WED 17

Horse mackerel, the second way

Horse mackerel in two pieces, and sugata-zukuri: sashimi served in the shape of the fish. Sashimi, namerou, aji fry. Nasu no nibitashi. Monkfish liver.

THU 18

Hiroko serves a full service

The prep for a service, then Hiroko serves the whole counter while you watch. Abalone. Vegan sushi.

FRI 19

The final service agreed

Prep in the morning. Then every chef serves a full omakase at the counter, in two rounds, the others as guests. The afternoon is for repeating any technique the chefs ask for.

Hiroko Ishii.

Founder of Japan Culinary Masters Academy in Otsuka, Tokyo, where she teaches sushi in English. A degree in food science, Le Cordon Bleu in Paris, the Tokyo Sushi Academy, then nine years as a sushi chef and instructor in Switzerland.

She set this schedule herself, day by day, and will stay in the room until five for anyone who keeps practising. Check her at jcma.tokyo, and on Instagram at @sushiroco.

The terms, plainly.

  • For cooks who cook for a living, on a yacht or in a private house, and have been asked for sushi.
  • Three weeks, as one programme. We do not offer a shorter one.
  • Lunch is included on every class day.
  • It runs from six chefs. Below six it does not run, and nobody pays for a programme that does not happen.
  • Nothing is on sale. Those who raised a hand hear everything in writing before anything is asked of them.
  • You can go direct. Her courses are on jcma.tokyo. What we add is a room of working chefs, and everything around the lessons.
