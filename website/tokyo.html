Learn omakase counter service in front of your guests.

Taught byHiroko IshiiFounder, Japan Culinary Masters Academy, Tokyo

Three weeks in Tokyo. Rice, the knife, the fish, and the order of a full omakase, with your hands, in English. You finish by standing behind the counter and serving.

Mon 1 – Fri 19 March 2027 Otsuka, Tokyo, in English From six chefs

Not on sale yet. The programme is being finished with Hiroko. Raise your hand and you hear first.

What you learn

Run the counter,
not just the fish.

Guests ask for omakase. A counter, a handful of guests, one piece at a time, nothing to hide behind. It rests on rice, a knife and timing, and none of that comes from a video. So the three weeks go in three steps.

01

Make it

Cook and season the rice. Form nigiri. Sharpen and use the yanagiba and deba. Break down whole fish, with the skin off by the knife. Do ikejime. Age a bream. Cure with kelp.

02

Build it

See how a full omakase is put together: the order of the pieces, the timing, what is prepared ahead and what is done in front of the guest.

03

Serve it

Stand behind the counter and serve, piece by piece, to the other chefs. Then sit on the other side. Turn and turn about, with a sushi chef watching.

What you come out with

By the last day you will have done this.

  • Cooked sushi rice and formed nigiri, every week.
  • Broken down a whole fish with the yanagiba and the deba, and a whole salmon, a whole yellowtail and a block of tuna.
  • Done ikejime on a sea bream, if you chose to.
  • Aged a bream for four days and tasted it against a fresh one.
  • Made the tamago on the pan you will use at work, and sharpened your own knives.
  • Rolled hosomaki, futomaki and inside-out rolls, and made tempura and simmered fish.
  • Served an omakase counter, and sat as a guest at the others’.
  • Spent three weeks beside chefs who work on boats and in private houses.

The three weeks

Week one builds your hands.
Week two builds the counter.
Week three goes deeper.

Monday to Friday, 9:30 to 15:00 in three sessions. Through the weeks the chefs take turns serving each other, one behind the counter and the others in front, so everyone practises both sides. Then the room is yours until 17:00, with Hiroko in it. Weekends free.

Week one

1 – 5 March

MON 1

Rice, and the first nigiri

Cook the rice, season it with sushi vinegar, form the first nigiri. The rice is most of what you taste, so it comes first and comes back on day two. Nikiri soy and ponzu. Knife check, and sharpening: she insists on it.

TUE 2

The yanagiba, and nori

Rice again, until it is steady. The yanagiba: slicing fish for sushi in one pull. Nori, and the first hosomaki.

WED 3

The deba, shrimp, dashi

The deba knife and three ways with shrimp. Nigiri from them, and miso soup from their heads. Basic dashi.

THU 4

A whole fish, and the tamago

Horse mackerel in three pieces, sanmai-oroshi, and the skin off with the knife. Nigiri and sashimi from it, a tartare, sangayaki. Then the egg omelette, made by every chef on the non-stick pan you will use at work.

FRI 5

A bench day

Hiroko is filling it, now that the market has moved to Saturday morning.

Week two

8 – 12 March

MON 8

Salmon, scallop, squid

Each from fresh or from frozen, since frozen is what a boat gets. Nigiri with each, the inside-out roll, nanban-zuke. Katsuramuki, the long peel of daikon, and a sashimi plate of three.

TUE 9

Ageing and curing

Sea bream aged four days, tasted against fresh. Yushimo, hot water on the skin. Kombu-jime, fish cured between sheets of kelp. Gizzard shad and mackerel. Conger eel, shown by Hiroko.

WED 10

Tuna

A block broken down. Zuke, tuna marinated in soy. Slicing for sushi and for sashimi. A tuna plate: nigiri, a mid-thick roll, a hand roll. Then the hot side: chawanmushi, tofu made from scratch, age-dashi, nasu no nibitashi.

THU 11

The counter starts

Simmered octopus. What is made ahead for a service, and in what order. Hiroko serves a full service. Then your first turn.

FRI 12

Three services

Three chefs behind the counter, each serving three guests. The rest sit, eat and judge. Then swap. Everyone takes both sides.

Week three

15 – 19 March

MON 15

Flounder and horse mackerel

Flounder. Horse mackerel in two pieces, and aji fry. Sugata-zukuri: sashimi served in the shape of the fish.

TUE 16

Whole salmon, whole yellowtail

From the fish to the piece. Saikyo-miso marinated fish for the grill.

WED 17

Shellfish

Abalone. Squid and cuttlefish. Ark shell, geoduck, surf clam. Monkfish liver.

THU 18

Rolls and rice

Futomaki and its fillings: shiitake, kanpyo, oboro. Softshell crab roll. Inari. Vegan sushi. Gari made from scratch.

FRI 19

Tempura, and simmered fish

Tempura. Nitsuke. Saba no miso-ni, mackerel simmered in miso. Then the last night.

Week three is built from Hiroko’s own list of what two weeks leave out. The order of its days is ours for now; she sets the final one.

An extra day, if you want it

Sushi for 12 guests. asked of Hiroko

The counter serves six, in the evening, piece by piece. Most weeks the ask is different: sushi for lunch, twelve guests at a table, and you still have dinner to cook. One more day, outside the three weeks, to learn to serve that.

TEMAKI

Hand rolls

Nori, rice and a filling, made in front of the guests and eaten within seconds. No plating.

CHIRASHI

The bowl

Sushi rice with the fish laid over it, one bowl per guest. Nothing to form by hand.

OSHIZUSHI

Pressed sushi

Pressed in a box and cut. It can be made ahead and carried to the table.

Priced separately, not part of the three weeks. The day is set with Hiroko once she has agreed.

Who teaches it

Hiroko Ishii.

Hiroko founded Japan Culinary Masters Academy in Otsuka, Tokyo, and teaches sushi there in English. A degree in food science, Le Cordon Bleu in Paris, the Tokyo Sushi Academy, then nine years as a sushi chef and instructor in Switzerland, 2009 to 2018.

She wrote this schedule herself, for yacht chefs. She is in the room every day, and stays until 17:00 for anyone who wants to keep practising. Check her at jcma.tokyo.

Around the three weeks

Where strangers become friends.

In Barcelona the chefs arrived as strangers and left as a group. Their notes are on the collaborations page. It happened at the table and on the walks as much as at the bench, so Tokyo gets the same care around the lessons.

The first table and the last dinner are on us. The rest is marked for what it is: being arranged, or still an idea.

SUN 28 FEB

The first table

One long table the night before day one. A plate and a drink, no speeches, no round of introductions. You walk into Monday already knowing four names.

3 WEEKS

One roof idea

A place near the school where chefs stay together, each in a room of their own. People who share a kitchen at breakfast do not need introducing. Otherwise you book your own.

SAT 6 MAR

Toyosu at dawn being arranged

The fish market with Hiroko: the tuna auction, then breakfast at the stalls behind the famous queues, which serve the same fish without the wait. Ikejime on a sea bream, your own, if you want to. Nobody is made to.

A NIGHT

Sake idea

A tasting with food, with someone who knows it. Sake is brewed in the cold months, so March is the end of the season.

A WEEKEND

A wasabi farm in Izu idea

A day out of Tokyo to where wasabi grows in running spring water. Pulled, grated, eaten. Most of us have only ever had the paste.

A NIGHT

A tempura or soba counter idea

Sushi is one of the Edo counter crafts. Tempura and soba are two more. An evening at a counter for each, to see how a room built around one thing does it.

SAT 13 MAR

Kappabashi being arranged

Tokyo’s kitchen-tool street, with Hiroko. Knives, pans, the small tools you only find here.

A NIGHT

Dinner at a counter, with Hiroko idea

The whole class at a sushi counter she chooses. To sit on the other side, watch a room that does this every night, and ask her about it over the pieces.

A NIGHT

The tuna night being arranged

A whole bluefin of around 30 kg, taken apart by a tuna wholesaler with the long knife, while Hiroko explains each cut for working chefs. With sake. She is asking her supplier how to do it.

FRI 19 MAR

The last night

A long dinner, all of us, with the roles swapped one last time. The people you cooked for, and who cooked for you, around one table.

Who it is for

Cooks who cook for a living,
and have been asked for sushi.

Yes, if you
  • Cook on a yacht or in a private house, now or soon
  • Have been asked for omakase, or sushi for the table, and want to do it properly
  • Can be in Tokyo for all three weeks, 1 to 19 March 2027. It is three weeks or nothing
  • Will cook, correct and be corrected beside other chefs
Not this one, if you
  • Have never cooked professionally. Everyone in the room does it for a living
  • Want to watch. Here the hands do the work
The honest terms

Straight, like a good galley.

  • Nothing is on sale. The programme comes first. Those who raised a hand hear everything in writing before anything is asked of them.
  • Three weeks, as one programme. It is built to be done whole, so there is no two-week version.
  • It runs from six chefs. Hiroko’s number, to keep the counter practice real. Below six it does not run, and nobody pays for a week that does not happen.
  • You can go direct. Hiroko teaches at her own school; her courses are on jcma.tokyo. What we add is the room of working cooks, the third week built for them, and everything around the lessons.
  • A plan, not a promise. Where it says “being arranged” or “idea”, that is what it is.
