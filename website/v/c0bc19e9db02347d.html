Obata Shuzo — Gakkogura

Sake brewing · Sado Island, Niigata, Japan

We keep a free record of where crafts are still taught at the source. We would like to put you on it, and we would rather you saw it first. Nothing below is published yet.

Nobody pays to be on the record — no advertising, no commission, no booking fee — and sending us a correction will not move you up it.

What we would publish

We name no teacher — we could not establish one

  • A working sake brewery inside a primary school that closed in 2010 and was converted in 2014.
  • Their own condition of entry: anyone may come and make sake provided they can stay on the island at least one week.
  • Participants join the brewers; time outside the work is their own.
  • Their own page says many people come to Gakkogura, including foreigners.
  • Obata Shuzo has brewed MANOTSURU on Sado since 1892.
  • The school stood on the hill for the sunset, and closed in 2010 as the island's population fell.
  • Their motto is shi-ho-wa-jo, four treasures in harmony: the rice, the water, the brewers and Sado itself.

What we could not work out

  • Are there fixed dates, or is it arranged case by case?
  • Is there a cost, and what does it cover?
  • Is the teaching in Japanese, or is English possible?
  • Who would a visiting chef actually be working beside — can we name them?

These are printed because they are the honest state of what we know. If you answer them we will say so, with the date.

And photographs, if you would

The photograph of the old schoolhouse under the cherry blossom, on your Gakkogura page. It says in one frame what a paragraph cannot: a school that became a brewery.

If you have others, these are the five that would let a chef see what the week actually is — one of each is plenty:

  • The place. The building, the room, the town it sits in.
  • Whoever teaches. At work rather than posed, if there is a choice.
  • The technique. Hands doing the thing, close.
  • People learning. The bench with somebody at it. Only if the people in it have agreed — we would rather have no picture than one somebody did not consent to.
  • The one move. The gesture that is the craft — the thing a stranger would recognise it by.

We will not take one off your website. Photographs are yours, and the only ones on our record are the ones a school chose and sent. You pick them, you tell us how to credit them, and you see where they go before they go anywhere. Sending them does not move you up the record — nothing does.

If it is easier: tell us we may use what you have already posted, and we will credit it to you and link back. That costs you nothing to find.
