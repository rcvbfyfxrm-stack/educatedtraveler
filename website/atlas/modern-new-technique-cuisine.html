Catalogue of Skills / Kitchen & Cellar

New culinary techniques & technologies

For professional chefs

The movement whose own masters buried its most famous name — while its technique quietly took over the best kitchens on earth.

Spherification, foams, sous-vide, liquid nitrogen: real chemistry that became standard in fine dining — and a marketing phrase, "molecular gastronomy," that the very chefs it's pinned on publicly rejected.

This is the toolkit on its own, learned at a lab, and it long ago stopped being Spanish — the labs here are in Barcelona, Paris, Melbourne and Puebla. The cuisine it came out of, and the lineage still cooking it in Spain, is Modernist Spanish Cuisine →

A working modernist-technique lab run by chef Martin Lippo, in the heart of the living scene. The curriculum is the real thing, module by module — vacuum & sous-vide, foams and carbonation, spherification (basic and advanced), liquid nitrogen, textures — and enrolment is genuinely open: buy an online module outright, or book an in-person class. Not a gatekept stage, not a museum ticket — a place you can actually walk into and cook.

On languageThe online courses run in English; the in-person Barcelona classes are taught in Spanish (English or French only as a private booking). The Basque Culinary Center's "Molecular Cooking" course also runs online in English — so an English speaker has a real route in without leaving the technique behind.

In your futureFine dining now assumes this vocabulary. A cook who genuinely understands spherification, precise temperature and hydrocolloids — not as tricks but as tools — carries a portable, in-demand skill into any serious kitchen.

The courses, and what they cost
  • Online modules (in English): Foams, Sous-Vide, Spherification (basic & advanced), Liquid Nitrogen, Textures — individual modules roughly €85–95 plus theory, or discounted full packs (€210–€552). Bought directly, by anyone.
  • In-person, Barcelona: booked from the catalogue or by arranging a private topic; prices on enquiry; Spanish (English/French privately).

Also real, if not the pick: the Basque Culinary Center in San Sebastián — an institutional heavyweight with avant-garde and "Molecular Cooking" courses (some in English) and open-enrolment short courses. The Upper Room / Sous Vide Australia in Melbourne teaches in English, but leans sous-vide rather than the full modernist range. Le Cordon Bleu Paris runs four days of this exact toolkit — sous-vide, molecular and cryogenic, espumas, smoking — designed by Éric Briffard, Meilleur Ouvrier de France, and "taught in French and consecutively translated into English"; two years in a professional kitchen gets you in without their diploma. It is the closest thing to a like-for-like alternative to the week below, and it publishes neither a price nor a date, so both are an enquiry. Not schools, honestly: elBulli1846 is a museum you visit, not a place you study; and El Celler de Can Roca's "stage" is a selective, gatekept placement — they pick you from a CV and a photo — not a course you can book.

Catalogued, not checked

Nine more places we know of, and nobody has checked any of them

Listing five places would tell you this craft happens in five places, and it does not. These are the rest we know of. Nobody has checked any of them. No school here is vetted, no teacher named, nothing graded — this is the lowest state on the map, and we would rather print it than let a short list read as a complete one. A line each, and what the place is known for. If you have been to one, or know a school or an instructor there, the line will take a note — it comes to me, and it is how a place stops being a line and starts being checked.

  • Copenhagen, Denmark
    Fermentation and preservation developed as a technical discipline with published method.
  • Lima, Peru
    Where technique meets an ingredient map most kitchens have never worked with.
  • São Paulo, Brazil
    Amazonian ingredients handled with modern technique, and a serious research kitchen culture.
  • Singapore, Singapore
    A dense high-end restaurant scene where equipment and technique move fast.
  • Tokyo, Japan
    Precision technique from an entirely different tradition, and the equipment industry behind it.
  • London, United Kingdom
    A development-kitchen culture and the easiest English-language access to it.
  • New York, United States
    Where much of the equipment and food-science literature is written and taught.
  • Bangkok, Thailand
    A modern Thai kitchen scene applying these tools to a non-European larder.
  • Dubai, United Arab Emirates
    A fast-growing restaurant market that hires internationally and pays for technique.

Four named masters running four real labs on four continents — the strongest teaching on this map that belongs to no ground at all, which is the whole point of a toolkit.

How this was graded

●●●○○

Three of the five answers are yes here

The same five questions, asked of every craft on this map, before we send anyone anywhere. A full dot is a yes we can show you the working for. An empty dot means one of three things, and the sentence under it says which: nobody publishes the answer, we read it and the answer is no, or nobody of ours has been.

● Is there a teacher, and can we name them?Yes, in all four places, and each is a named living person teaching in their own lab: Martin Lippo at Vakuum in Barcelona, Éric Briffard at Le Cordon Bleu Paris, Miguel Ángel Cepeda in Puebla and Mexico City, Dale Prentice at Sous Vide Australia in Lilydale. That is four out of four, which nothing else on this map manages. ⛔ Where we stand: EducatedTraveler runs a paid week with Martin Lippo, so we are not a neutral party about him. Nobody pays to be on this map and no money reaches us from him; the way in without us is his own site, martinlippo.com.

● Can a stranger get in, on a date you can book?Open, and unusually so for the avant-garde. This is the rare corner of modernist cooking you can enrol in rather than apply for a stage at: open courses and private masterclasses at Vakuum, a four-day 31-hour Advanced Culinary Techniques course in Paris taught with English translation as it is given, a multi-week diplomado in Mexico, public workshops in Melbourne. And the hands can ruin it — a spherification bath and liquid nitrogen are unforgiving, which is why the labs are labs.

● Will there be other people learning beside you?Yes, on the open courses and the diplomado, which run as classes rather than as private hire. What is not published anywhere is the group size at any of the four, and in a technique lab that is the number that decides whether you touch the equipment or watch it. Ask before you book.

○ Is the craft alive in this place, or is the school the only thing here?No, and it is worth saying why rather than treating it as a hole. The test this question applies is whether the craft, taught this way, would make sense anywhere else — and a technique toolkit is designed to travel. Sous-vide was worked out at Troisgros in 1974 and now belongs to every kitchen with a circulator. Catalonia's claim on the avant-garde is real and it is graded on this map's modernist Spanish cuisine page, where the restaurants are. Here you are buying a technique, and the technique is portable by design.

○ Is there enough here to keep you learning for years?Nobody of ours has been, and this craft has no ladder to point at instead: its own record says there is no single formal body and that the qualification is a certificate of attendance. That is not a criticism of the labs, which are teaching real skills. It does mean that what happens after the course is entirely up to you and your own kitchen.

Four is the ceiling here, not five: the fourth answer is a settled no, because this is a toolkit rather than a tradition, and portability is what makes it worth learning.

Checked and not listed

What did not clear · 9

Searched in 5 of 11 regions of the world, on 2026-09-01. Everything below is real, and none of it is on this map. The reason sits next to each one, because a place left off without a reason is just an opinion.

  • Espai Sucre ↗ Barcelona, Spain
    Closed. Jordi Butrón announced in April 2024 that the last class had been taught and the doors shut on 31 July 2024, on his cardiologist's advice. Its site is now a blank page. It was listed here until 1 September 2026 as 'operating and enrollable', which was wrong for over two years — the night check found the dead certificate and the press confirmed the rest.
  • elBulli1846 (elBullifoundation) ↗ Roses / Cala Montjoi, Spain
    A museum. Their own pages sell a ticket and a visit of about two and a half hours; there is no enrolment, no kitchen you cook in and no teacher.
  • El Celler de Can Roca ↗ Girona, Spain
    A stage, not a course. They pick you from a CV and a photograph, on their timetable, and no amount of money changes that. Real, and not something a stranger can book.
  • Disfrutar ↗ Barcelona, Spain
    The world's best restaurant in 2024, run by three cooks out of elBulli — and a restaurant. You can eat there. There is nothing to enrol in.
  • Modernist Cuisine Lab (Nathan Myhrvold) ↗ Bellevue, Washington, United States
    The lab behind the field's reference book — a research kitchen, a photography studio and a machine shop, with occasional private dinners. It teaches through the books and the gallery, not through a room you can join.
  • Fundació Alícia ↗ Sant Fruitós de Bages, Spain
    A food-science research centre with real teaching, but its workshops are designed on request for companies and groups. Nothing an individual can put a date on, which is the line this map draws.
  • Paco Roncero Taller ↗ Madrid, Spain
    A multisensory research and dining space opened in 2012, where the room rains on you while you eat. It develops dishes and serves them. It is not a course.
  • MOM Culinary Institute ↗ Madrid, Spain
    Two-year vocational degrees in cuisine and restaurant management, opened 2021 with Paco Roncero's name on the door. A hospitality school rather than a modernist one, and the named chef is the patron, not the teacher at your bench.
  • FERRANDI Paris · ALMA (Colorno) · Culinary Institute of America ↗ France, Italy, United States
    Three of the strongest schools in the world, checked together because they fail on the same point: their published catalogues are classical French, Italian and American programmes. Nothing dedicated to modernist technique that a working professional can book short. Worth re-checking — a school this size can add one in a season.

Why you can trust this page

How a place gets on here, and what we do when it stops being true

There is a real difference between a school that teaches you and a good-looking website, and too much of the internet is the second kind. Four instruments do the work, and every one of them is visible on the page you are already on.

  • We grade it in public, and publish what is missing. The Measure is five named conditions, not a score: the room, the door, the bench, the ground, the stretch. An empty dot tells you which one is missing and why. A craft nobody has graded shows no meter at all, because an absence is not a zero.
  • We print what we checked and did NOT list. Every swept craft carries the places we looked at and turned down, each with its reason. That list is the part no marketplace prints, because a rejection is where the money is.
  • The claims are read back to their sources every night. A robot re-reads every link we publish against the school's own page each night, and every price and course name we have written a test for. When one stops matching three nights running, it is flagged for a human — and an entry that cannot be confirmed comes off. It is never softened.
  • Where we are not neutral, we say so above the recommendation. We run one paid week a year, with one teacher. His entry says that before it says anything good about him, and prints the way to reach him without us, at his prices.
  • Nothing here has been stood in yet. An open sheet means desk research, carefully done. No place on this map has been visited and dated by us. When one is, the check will carry a name and a date — and until then, the grade says so in the dots it leaves empty.
  • Nobody pays to be here, and nobody can pay to be left off. No commission, no listing fee, no tourist board. The order is the strength of the community, never the size of the wallet.
  • And for this craft in particular: we say which kind of "modernist" a place teaches — the real technique, or the spectacle the founders themselves disowned. That distinction is the whole craft, and it is the first thing a school will blur.
Before you trust any school — mine or anyone else's — ask these five things
  1. Who actually teaches it? Can you find them by name, with a track record you can check yourself?
  2. Is the craft alive in that place, or is the school the only thing there? A real scene has more than one good option.
  3. What exactly do you walk away with — a recognised qualification, or a certificate they printed themselves? Ask which.
  4. Can you speak to someone who did the course? A real person, not a testimonial on their own page.
  5. What happens on a bad day — weather, an injury, a teacher who doesn't show? A serious place has an honest answer.

If a place dodges these, that is your answer. It costs you nothing to ask, and it tells you everything.
