Catalogue of Skills / Kitchen & Cellar

Being a chef at sea

For cooks going aboard, and chefs already there

You are usually the only cook aboard. One person running a whole department: you order weeks of food before you leave, feed the same fixed group three times a day, and do it in a room that does not stay level. When it goes wrong there is nobody to send out for anything.

Nobody's country decides whether you're qualified to do it. The flag on the transom does — and it usually asks for less than the schools do.

Where it is most alive

Not a school. A dock, in a season.

This craft is alive in four ports and two months of the year, and everywhere else it is quiet. Antibes and Palma from spring, where the Mediterranean's crew are actually hired. Fort Lauderdale and the Caribbean from autumn, when the fleet crosses. Turn up in the right port in the right month and the whole trade is standing on the quay — the boats, the agencies, the chefs coming off a season and the ones going on.

Which is the honest first instruction: go to the dock before you buy anything. If you want a room and a teacher first, there are three ways in and one of them costs nothing but paperwork.

More about the craftWhy there is nothing here to inherit — no old workshop, no line of teachers, no master.

This is a trade, not a tradition. Thousands do it for a living, and nobody hands it down: there is no old workshop and no line of teachers. It passes between chefs who overlap for a season, aboard — which is why the people worth learning from are working chefs who decided to teach, not heirs to anything.

It is also why this sheet has no master on it. On most crafts in the Atlas we would name the person you go and stand next to. Here that person is whoever is in the next galley when you get there.

01 · Where we'd send you

Three routes — and the third is only paperwork

With no lineage to apprentice into, what there is — and it counts — is two working yacht chefs who came off the boats to teach their own trade, under their own names, on published dates. The third route is not a school at all.

What we could not establish is Anchor & Apron's room. Their pages say the exact venue goes to students two weeks before the week starts, so from the outside we cannot tell whether the teaching kitchen is theirs year-round or taken for the fortnight. That is an open question, not a mark against them — and it is the sort of thing we would rather leave open than guess at.

Route one — the ticket

Assessment in Marine Cookery, at an MCA-approved centre

England & Scotland · in English · several centres, not one

Three to five days of assessment. It is not a school's award: passing it is one of four things the MCA wants before it issues the Ship's Cook Certificate of Competency — the others being food-safety training, the five STCW basics and an ENG1 medical. That certificate is the document no flag will argue with.

The part nobody says out loud: it only counts at a centre the MCA has approved, and that list changes. On the most recent list we could find — an MCA reply reproduced publicly, accurate as of summer 2023 — the approved centres were Leiths School of Food and Wine (London), City of Glasgow College, Dumfries and Galloway College (Stranraer) and Entier Ltd (Westhill, Aberdeenshire). Tante Marie (Woking) advertises the assessment on its own current pages but is not on that 2023 list. We cannot resolve that from a desk, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

The gate18+ · competent spoken and written English and numeracy · a professional-kitchen track record. Leiths asks for a CV, covering letter and passport copy; Tante Marie asks for a Level 2 cookery qualification. Closed to a career-changer with no kitchen behind them.
Go direct — and what it costsConfirm a centre's current approval with the MCA before you pay anyone — mlc@mcga.gov.uk, who hold the only list that is actually current. Best-documented centre we found: Leiths, London — three days, £1,045 inc VAT, dates published into 2027, admissions@leiths.com. Also Tante Marie, Woking — Level 2 Food Safety £105 + VAT, Level 3 Supervising Food Safety £395 + VAT, full fee on their guidelines table. The certificate comes from the MCA, not any school — form MSF 4395. We take nothing from any of it.
Route two — the orientation

Yacht Chef Training — Anchor & Apron

Mallorca, Spain · in English · named teachers

Two weeks with Cara Whiteman and Amber Colle, working yacht chefs who teach it themselves and publish their own backgrounds. Galley management, provisioning, menus, etiquette and deck duties — plus three days and nights aboard a catamaran, which is the part that matters and the part almost no classroom has.

The gate — and the honest limitsNo experience needed, so the door is genuinely open. But it does not produce a Ship's Cook Certificate; STCW and a medical are separate, compulsory and not included; and no school can promise a job — theirs says a first boat "could take 2 weeks to 3 months." Cancellation, in their own words: "within 12 weeks of training start date full refund minus €800 deposit", "within 8 weeks 50% refund", "anything less there is no refund". As drafted those two windows overlap, so settle which one applies to your date with them before you pay the deposit.
Go direct — and what it costsanchorandapron.co/training · hello@anchorandapron.co · €3,950 all-in for two weeks, next run 2–16 May 2027. The advanced week — one week, villa only, for chefs already working — is €3,200; it runs 24 September to 1 October 2026, and their page says that one is full and points to 2027. Instalments scale with how far ahead you book, four months out in four parts, three in three, and their own note says a plan comes to slightly more than paying in full. Accommodation, meals and transfers in; flights out. Nothing comes back to us if you go.
Route three — the paper

Recognition of Qualification as a Ship's Cook — Cayman Islands

By email · in English · no course, no travel, no assessment

A Cayman document, for Cayman vessels — read that first, because everything below depends on it. If you already cook for a living it is also the route easiest to miss: there is no course attached to it, so nobody has a reason to sell it to you. You send Cayman copies of what you already hold, and they send a document back. There is nothing to attend and nothing to sit.

The order of things is the useful part. Cayman's own guidance says a chef holding those qualifications is already entitled to serve as a ship's cook on a Cayman vessel. The document does not grant that — your certificates did. On its face it is one page instead of a folder. Whether a captain, a manager or a surveyor ever asks to see it, we have not established. Proof, not permission.

What it is notIt is not a Ship's Cook Certificate, and Cayman never calls it one. Their own word for it is voluntary — "issued on a voluntary basis and… not required by either the Regulations or MLC 2006 itself." It is a Cayman document for Cayman vessels. We have not established that any other flag accepts it, so do not plan on it travelling — and we did not check whether any other registry issues a document like this one.
Go direct — and what you sendForm CISR 3910, with copies of: your professional cookery qualification · food hygiene and handling at managerial level · a CV evidencing the experience you claim · your passport, signature page included · STCW basic training, all four certificates or a Certificate of Proficiency, with refresher evidence from the last five years · a seafarer medical with at least three months still to run on the day you apply. Scanned PDFs to technical@cishipping.com, whole email under 5 MB. The form carries a company countersignature marked "where applicable", which reads as though an individual may apply alone. We have not had that confirmed — ask technical@cishipping.com before you assume it. Valid five years. There is a fee, and the form does not state it — Cayman invoices once the application is processed. We did not find the amount published anywhere; ask before you start. Guidance Note 02/2018 · the form itself. We take nothing from any of it.

02 · Before you pay anyone

Do you need a qualification at all?

Three questions decide it. Most people never ask the first one.

Who eats your food?
Guests onlyIn Cayman's own words, a visiting chef who cooks only for non-crew is not a ship's cook at all. None of this describes you.
The crewYou are the ship's cook in law. Keep going.
How many seafarers does she ordinarily carry — and what does your flag count as "enough"?
Fewer than tenOn Cayman, the Marshall Islands and the UK, no qualified cook is required — but the number belongs to the flag, and the Isle of Man starts at six. Read your own notice before you rely on this. Anyone handling food still needs hygiene, handling and storage training.
Ten or more, master includedA fully qualified cook must be carried. Keep going.
Where does she actually go?
Short hopsCayman lets it go under three days and within 36 hours of a safe port. The UK draws its line at 60 miles from a safe haven, no foreign port call.
Real passagesYou need it — and the only question left is what your flag counts as a qualification.

"Qualified" is not one thing. It means something different on every transom.

And if the answer was yes — the six documents

Our order, not a flag's — the order they bite in. Not one of the six is the thing sold as a career, and three of them expire: the two STCW refreshers, and the medical.

01

STCW basic training. Four certificates under STCW A-VI/1: personal survival techniques, fire prevention and fire fighting, elementary first aid, personal safety and social responsibility. This is the floor for working aboard at all, before any question of cooking arises. Where this page says "the five", that is the four plus security awareness — how the schools sell it as a package; four is what the convention names. Cayman asks, under A-VI/1.3, for evidence that personal survival and fire prevention and fire fighting have been refreshed within the last five years. Those two are the ones that lapse.

02

A seafarer medical. The ENG1 under the UK; elsewhere one meeting MLC 2006 Standard A1.2 or STCW Regulation I/9. Cayman will not take one with less than three months left to run on the day you apply, which is worth knowing before you post an application against an expiring certificate.

03

Food safety — and this is the one that catches people. Level 2 is the floor for handling food and it is what most chefs hold. Cayman's checklist asks for food hygiene and handling "at managerial level" without naming a level; in UK terms that is usually read as Level 3, and we have not had Cayman confirm it. Ask them what they will accept before you buy a course — and note Cayman also says that where the training is not named on your certificate you must send separate evidence of it, such as the syllabus.

04

A professional cookery qualification — or the years that stand in for one. An NVQ Level 2, City & Guilds 706, or a diploma from a shore-based school, provided that school sits in a country on Cayman's Shipping Notice 05/2011. In its place, Cayman will take confirmation that you have served as a chef in a professional kitchen, commercial yacht or passenger ship; the MCA will take twelve months in a catering establishment (MSN 1846, read 22 August and not re-read since). This is the door the self-taught walk through, and route three above is what it opens onto.

05

Sea service. The MCA wants one month, in any capacity — deckhand, stew, anything. Not one month as a cook. Cayman's checklist asks for no sea time, only a CV that evidences the experience you are claiming.

06

A passport, signature page included. Named here only because it is its own line on Cayman's checklist, under a form that warns in capitals: "failure to do so may mean that we will have to return your application to you." It is not queried. It is sent back.

What is not on this listA superyacht chef course. A house certificate. A GUEST or IYT unit. No flag on this page asks for any of them by name. Cookery schools do issue some of what is on the list — an NVQ, a City & Guilds, a food-safety level, the marine cookery assessment itself. What a flag recognises is the qualification, never the school that sold it.
03–05 · The detail, if you want it

Open what you need

Four flags, four answersCayman will take your CV. The Marshall Islands names no certificate. The UK wants a Certificate of Competency. The Isle of Man starts counting at six.

It all comes from the ILO's Maritime Labour Convention 2006 — a seafarers' welfare instrument, which is why the trigger counts crew and not tonnage. Then each registry writes its own version.

Cayman Islands

Your CV can be the qualification

Ten or more seafarers, on voyages over three days or more than 36 hours from a safe port.

"Qualified" can be met by an NVQ Level II — or simply confirmation that you have served as a chef in a professional kitchen, commercial yacht or passenger ship, plus hygiene, storage, stock-control and health-and-safety training.

They will also issue a Recognition of Qualification as a Ship's Cook on request — voluntary, their word, and not a Ship's Cook Certificate. It is route three, and it is the only one of the three that is paperwork alone.

Marshall Islands

Competent, and documented

The regulation is short: cooks "shall be at least 18 years of age and documented as competent for their positions."

No certificate is named. The standard is delegated, not spelled out — the owner, the manager and the flag's guidance fill the gap.

United Kingdom · MCA

A formal Certificate of Competency

The strictest of the three, and the only one naming a document: the Ship's Cook Certificate of Competency.

Age 18, one month of sea service in any capacity, the five STCW basics, an ENG1. It replaced rules that once demanded five years at sea.

"A visiting chef who is on board who only prepares food for guests and other 'non crew members' is not considered as a ship's cook for the purposes of MLC 2006."Cayman Islands Shipping Registry, Guidance Note 02/2018
  • Cayman Islands — Shipping Notice 07/2014 Rev 3 §6, and Guidance Note 02/2018 (Rev 1.0), 16 March 2018. The recognition statement is "issued on a voluntary basis and… not required by either the Regulations or MLC 2006 itself."
  • Marshall Islands — MI-108, Rev. Mar/2023, §7.44.2.
  • United Kingdom — MSN 1846 (M). §2.1: "A qualified ship's cook must be carried on all ships when the total crew consists of 10 seafarers or more on-board (including the master or skipper)." Apply on form MSF 4395.
  • Isle of Man — Manx Shipping Notice MSN 052 (Minimum Safe Manning Requirements, Jan 2023) §6, read 31 August 2026: "A qualified ship's cook is required for vessels operating with a prescribed manning of 6 or more seafarers on voyages of more than 3 days or more than 36 hours from a safe port." Same convention, same voyage test, a different number — which is the whole point of this page. Detail in their MLN 3.2. MSN 052.
  • Not read, and named as unread: Malta, the Bahamas, Jersey and Gibraltar carry large yacht fleets too. Read your own flag's notice, or ask your captain for it. As the four answers above show, it changes.
  • All three documents read in full, 22 August 2026: CISN 07/2014 · MI-108 · MSN 1846 (M).
What's true, and what's soldNo flag requires a "superyacht chef course" — and the schools rarely have to say so.
What's true

The threshold is ten seafarers, master included — and most yachts run fewer.

On two of the three biggest yacht flags, professional kitchen time plus a hygiene qualification is the standard. No school required.

Under the UK's stricter route, the entry price is one month at sea in any capacity — not five years, and not a diploma.

What's sold

A ten-day or two-week "superyacht chef course" awarding a house certificate or an industry one — GUEST, IYT. Useful. No flag requires either.

Recruitment support and industry contacts. Real help, worth paying for. No school can promise a job, and the honest ones say so.

Remaining-places counts. We do not verify anyone's, here or anywhere.

The con is not a fake school. It is the slide from useful to required — and nobody arrives having read their own flag's notice.

So who can skip the certificate entirely?

You can skip itYou cook only for guests — Cayman does not count you as the ship's cook at all, and none of this is about you. Or she carries fewer than ten seafarers, though check your own flag for the number: the Isle of Man draws its line at six, not ten. Or you already hold a professional cookery qualification and managerial food hygiene and she flies Cayman — their guidance says you are entitled to the job already, and route three only puts that on one page. On the Marshall Islands no certificate is named at all: the standard is delegated, so it is your owner or manager who decides, not a document.
You cannotShe is UK-flagged and over the threshold: the MCA names a document, and we found no substitute route in MSN 1846 — if you hold a foreign certificate, ask mlc@mcga.gov.uk before assuming it carries. Or you are changing careers with no professional kitchen behind you — the assessment's own gate is closed to you, and a route that trades a qualification for recognition needs the qualification first. For you none of the three is open yet, and no course changes that. The first step is kitchen time — the certificate comes after it, not instead of it.
Three things nobody tells youFive years at sea became one month. The legal ceiling is a Level 2.
01

Cook only for guests and the law doesn't call you the ship's cook. MLC regulates whoever feeds the crew — which on plenty of boats is not the person with "chef" on the crew list.

02

Five years at sea became one month. In any capacity. The trigger also stopped being tonnage and operating area and became, in the MCA's own words, "the number of crew carried on the ship."

03

The legal ceiling is a Level 2. The UK pegs the outcomes no higher than QCF Level 2; Cayman names an NVQ Level II. The law that governs feeding a ship sits below a culinary diploma.

What no certificate testsThe floor working against you — and the one question we haven't asked a teacher yet.
The floor working against you

Which pan gets a wet cloth, how full is too full, where you stand when she rolls, which knife cut you don't attempt on a beam sea. The variable is the swell, and the swell is not in the book.

An audience that cannot leave

The same faces, three meals a day, for weeks. The judgement being trained is not "is this good" — it is "is this the fourth time this week."

The mistake that ruins it — not established
