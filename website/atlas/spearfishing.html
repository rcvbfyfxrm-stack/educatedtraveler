Where else this craft lives · ten

Listing five places would tell you this craft happens in five places, and it does not. These are the rest we know of. Nobody has checked any of them. No school here is vetted, no teacher named, nothing graded — this is the lowest state on the map, and we would rather print it than let a short list read as a complete one. A line each, and what the place is known for.

  • Sardinia, Italy
    Rocky Tyrrhenian coast with one of Europe's deepest-rooted breath-hold hunting cultures.
  • Dalmatian islands, Croatia
    Clear Adriatic water, strict permit rules, and a serious domestic scene.
  • The Cyclades, Greece
    Island coastlines where spearfishing is ordinary practice rather than a holiday activity.
  • Azores, Portugal
    Mid-Atlantic pelagic hunting, weather-dependent and for experienced divers.
  • Cape Verde, Cape Verde
    Warm Atlantic bluewater with wahoo and tuna close to the islands.
  • Coiba & the Pacific coast, Panama
    Remote bluewater reached by boat, with strong protection rules around the park.
  • Cape Town & the Cape peninsula, South Africa
    Cold, tough Atlantic conditions and a demanding local tradition.
  • Northland, New Zealand
    A national spearfishing culture with well-defined size and bag limits.
  • Exmouth, Western Australia, Australia
    Ningaloo's edge, where reef and bluewater meet within a short run.
  • Florida Keys, United States
    Shallow reef and wreck hunting under tightly enforced seasonal rules.
Is this community worth the trip

Three of the five answers are yes here

The same five questions, asked of every craft on this map, before we send anyone anywhere. A full dot is a yes we can show you the working for. An empty dot means one of three things, and the sentence under it says which: nobody publishes the answer, we read it and the answer is no, or nobody of ours has been.

●●●○○

The oldest hunt on earth, and on this map mostly sold as a charter — a state diploma at one end, a guide and a boat at the other, nobody named at either, and one coast where the sport plainly lives.

○ Is there a teacher, and can we name them?Empty in all three places, and the page says so about itself: no single named regional club could be verified on the Côte d'Azur. Kona and Cabo name schools and charter operations rather than instructors. This craft is passed between hunters more than it is taught in classes, which makes the absence understandable and does not make it less of a gap.

● Can a stranger get in, on a date you can book?Open, at two very different levels. CREPS PACA at Antibes delivers the French state professional diploma for breath-hold disciplines — a real, examined qualification. Kona Freedivers runs a two-day spearfishing course that requires a Level 1 Freediver certification before you may book it, which is a door with a standard on it. And the hands can ruin it beyond argument: a speargun on a breath-hold, with a shot you either take cleanly or should not take.

● Will there be other people learning beside you?Partly, and only in one of the three. France's federated clubs are volunteer associations where members train and qualify together, and a state diploma at Antibes has a cohort by definition. Kona and Cabo sell private charters and one-on-one tours, which is the opposite — good instruction, no bench.

● Is the craft alive in this place, or is the school the only thing here?Yes, and settled by a federation that is not on this page and sells nothing. The Fédération Française de Pêche Sportive en Apnée ran the Challenge Nicolas Munier off Sainte-Maxime on 25 April 2026, on the Sardinaux plateau — the second round of its French Cup, 22 two-person teams, put on by its own Côte d'Azur regional league and doubling as a qualifier for the national team championship at Frontignan in June. A regional league running a national round on this coast is the sport belonging to the place rather than operating off it. What it does not settle is Kona or Cabo, where we still have nothing but sellers.Evidence, from people with nothing to sell you: the Fédération Française de Pêche Sportive en Apnée on its own Côte d'Azur cup round ↗ read 5 September 2026

○ Is there enough here to keep you learning for years?Nobody of ours has been. CMAS grades and the national federation licences are a real ladder, and the French diploma at Antibes is a genuine professional rung. Whether there is a community above you on any of these three coasts, rather than a boat you rent again, is not something a booking page can answer.

All five are reachable here — and the fourth answer, published empty the day this was signed, is now the strongest thing on the page. The gap was ours, not the coast's.

Why you can trust this map

How a place gets on here, and what we do when it stops being true

There is a real difference between a school that teaches you and a good-looking website, and too much of the internet is the second kind. Four instruments do the work, and every one of them is visible on the page you are already on.

  • We grade it in public, and publish what is missing. The Measure is five plain questions, not a score: is there a teacher we can name, can a stranger get in, will you be learning beside other people, is the craft alive in the place, and is there enough there to keep you going for years. An empty dot tells you which one we could not answer and why. A craft nobody has graded shows no meter at all, because an absence is not a zero.
  • We print what we checked and did NOT list. Every swept craft carries the places we looked at and turned down, each with its reason. That list is the part no marketplace prints, because a rejection is where the money is.
  • The claims are read back to their sources every night. A robot re-reads every link we publish against the school's own page each night, and every price and course name we have written a test for. page. When one stops matching three nights running, it is flagged for a human — and an entry that cannot be confirmed comes off. It is never softened.
  • Where we are not neutral, we say so above the recommendation. We run one paid week a year, with one teacher. His entry says that before it says anything good about him, and prints the way to reach him without us, at his prices.
  • Nothing here has been stood in yet. An open sheet means desk research, carefully done. No place on this map has been visited and dated by us. When one is, the check will carry a name and a date — and until then, the grade says so in the dots it leaves empty.
  • Nobody pays to be here, and nobody can pay to be left off. No commission, no listing fee, no tourist board. The order is the strength of the community, never the size of the wallet.
Before you trust any school — mine or anyone else's — ask these five things
  1. Who actually teaches it? Can you find them by name, with a track record you can check yourself?
  2. Is the craft alive in that place, or is the school the only thing there? A real scene has more than one good option.
  3. What exactly do you walk away with — a recognised qualification, or a certificate they printed themselves? Ask which.
  4. Can you speak to someone who did the course? A real person, not a testimonial on their own page.
  5. What happens on a bad day — weather, an injury, a teacher who doesn't show? A serious place has an honest answer.

If a place dodges these, that's your answer. It costs you nothing to ask, and it tells you everything.
