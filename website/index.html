What this is

EducatedTraveler is two things.

One: a catalogue that puts the spotlight on hands-on skills you could make a life of.

Two: the weeks I design with an instructor, for people who want a particular way to learn a craft.

Why EducatedTraveler

Four things you can check before you believe me.

  1. 01

    Nobody buys their way onto the catalogue.

    No listing fee, no placement, no commission. A craft is not on it because someone paid to be there — and it opens because someone asked for it.

  2. 02

    Every open craft names the room and the person.

    The school, who still teaches it by hand, what your hands actually do there — and a link straight to them, so you can go direct without me.

  3. 03

    When I can’t stand behind a pick, the page says so.

    Some crafts read “a place, not a pick — no school named”. Most are marked catalogued, not checked yet. A blank costs one entry; a bad recommendation costs the reason to believe the next one.

  4. 04

    The week itself is hands in.

    One bench, in a master’s own working room, beside a handful of people learning the same skill. Not a tour and not a demonstration — you do the work, and you can ruin the piece.

Lab Weeks

What a Lab Week is.

I design it with the person who still does that work by hand — an instructor, an expert, a master — and it runs in their own working room. One bench, a small group, hands in.

It is the one thing here that costs money. A week is confirmed at ten seats, or every euro goes back.

Who is behind this, and why →

A

Arnaud

Founder · I check every place myself

I grew up in the Jura — about as far from the sea as you can get in France. I left school at 18 to find out what the world was really about, and I've been crossing it by sea and by land ever since — fifteen years of that, ten of them cooking on yachts, learning from the masters I met along the way. Here is some of it.

I · The beginning

So I went.

Everyone expected university and a career. I finished a science baccalauréate, worked factory shifts until I had two thousand euros, and flew to Melbourne instead.

II · The kitchens

Every kitchen a classroom.

It started in a Darwin dishpit, the day the chef ran short-handed and put a knife in my hand. The Michelin star came years later. Every chef a teacher.

III · The sea

More than fifty thousand nautical miles behind me.

First classes in Sydney Bay, a place on regatta crews by asking at the dock, then oceans. The sea cares about judgment, not a résumé.

IV · The depths

The deeper I went, the quieter it got.

Freediving Mexican cenotes and the open blue, sharks in the Galápagos. Fear turned into respect.

V · The heights

Strangers turned into crew.

Annapurna at 4,130 m, passes across the Himalaya. The people on the trail taught me more than any classroom.

VI · The stillness

Handed, at the source.

Breathwork in Bali, Ayurveda in India — and in the Thar desert, the idea for all of this took shape.
